What Happens If I Get Into an Accident in the USA as a Canadian?

Published: April 14, 2026

Introduction

Getting into an accident is stressful enough on its own. Getting into one while you are in another country can make the situation feel even more overwhelming.

A lot of Canadians assume that once they cross into the United States, their insurance no longer applies, or that they are on their own if something happens.

In most cases, that is not true.

If you are a Canadian resident and get into an accident in the USA, your Canadian auto insurance policy will generally still respond, assuming you carry the proper coverages.

Understanding how that process works can make things a lot less confusing if you ever find yourself in that situation.

Does My Canadian Insurance Work in the United States?

Generally speaking, yes.

Most Canadian auto insurance policies extend coverage into the United States. That means if you are involved in an accident while visiting the US, you should still report the claim to your own insurance company.

This applies whether:

  • You are at fault
  • Another driver is at fault
  • Your vehicle is damaged while parked
  • Your loss falls under another covered peril

Your insurer still has a legal responsibility to respond to the claim based on the coverages on your policy.

One thing to keep in mind is that this usually applies to the United States, but not necessarily Mexico. Many Canadian policies do not automatically extend coverage there, so it is always worth checking your policy before travelling outside Canada.

What Coverages Apply in the USA?

The same coverages you carry in Canada generally follow you into the United States.

For example:

  • Collision coverage may respond to accident damage
  • Comprehensive coverage may respond to things like theft, vandalism, or certain non-collision losses
  • All perils coverage may apply depending on the circumstances

What matters most is whether you actually carry those coverages on your policy before the accident happens.

What Happens If I Am At Fault?

If you are at fault for the accident, your insurer will typically handle the claim the same way they would if the accident happened in Canada.

Your vehicle repairs would usually be handled under your own coverages, subject to your deductible and policy terms.

The insurer may then deal with the third party claim and any legal or financial exposure afterward through the normal claims process.

From the insured’s perspective, the process itself is often more familiar than people expect.

What Happens If I Am Not At Fault?

This is where a lot of Canadians get confused.

Even if the other driver is clearly at fault, your insurer may still repair your vehicle through your own policy first.

In many cases, you may still have to pay your deductible upfront.

Once the repairs are completed, your insurer can pursue recovery against the at-fault driver or their insurance company. This process is called subrogation.

If the insurer successfully recovers the money, they will usually reimburse your deductible afterward.

A lot of people expect the other insurance company to immediately take over everything, but in cross-border claims, it is often smoother and faster for your own insurer to handle the repairs first and recover the money later.

Why Accident Rules Feel Different in the USA

One thing worth understanding is that insurance systems are not identical everywhere.

Canadian drivers are used to certain claims processes and accident benefit structures that may not exist in the same way in different US states.

Each state has its own insurance laws, liability rules, and minimum coverage requirements.

Because of that, handling a cross-border claim can sometimes take longer or involve additional steps compared to a normal accident in Canada.

What You Should Do After the Accident

If you are involved in an accident in the USA, the best thing you can do is treat it seriously and document everything.

You should:

  • Exchange information with the other driver
  • Take photos of the scene and damages
  • Obtain witness information if possible
  • Contact local police if required
  • Report the accident to your insurer as soon as possible

The sooner your insurance company is involved, the easier the process usually becomes.

Final Thoughts

Getting into an accident in another country can feel intimidating, but most Canadian auto policies are designed to continue protecting you while driving in the United States.

The important thing is understanding that your own insurer is still usually your first point of contact, regardless of who caused the accident.

Your coverages, deductibles, and policy terms still matter, and the process often works more similarly to a Canadian claim than people expect.
