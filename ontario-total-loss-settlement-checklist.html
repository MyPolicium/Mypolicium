Ontario Total Loss Settlement Checklist: What to Review Before You Accept

Receiving a total loss offer can often feel rushed. When the adjuster calls, you may be dealing with the stress of your rental vehicle coverage ending, or your car may already be sitting at a tow yard or body shop.

Because of this pressure, many drivers simply accept the first offer they receive without fully understanding it. This checklist is designed to help you slow down. Before you sign any paperwork or agree to a settlement, take the time to review the most important details of your valuation to ensure it is accurate.

The Settlement Checklist

Review the following items on the valuation report provided by your insurance company. If you notice discrepancies, you may be able to ask for a review before accepting the offer.

  • Confirm the vehicle year, make, model, and trim: Ensure the exact trim level (e.g., LX vs. Touring) is correctly listed, as this significantly impacts the base value.
  • Confirm mileage is accurate: Check that the odometer reading used in the calculation matches your vehicle's actual mileage at the time of the loss.
  • Confirm options and packages: Verify that premium features like sunroofs, advanced safety packages, or upgraded audio systems are included in the valuation.
  • Review the condition rating: Adjusters assign a condition rating (e.g., average, above average). Ensure deductions for wear and tear are reasonable and not overly aggressive.
  • Check whether prior damage was applied: Ensure the insurer did not deduct value for damage caused by the accident itself, confusing it with unrelated prior damage.
  • Review the comparable vehicles used: Look at the vehicles the insurer used to establish a baseline value. Are they actually similar to yours in mileage and condition?
  • Compare dealer asking prices carefully: Remember that retail asking prices at dealerships often include markups, warranties, and reconditioning fees that are not included in actual cash value.
  • Confirm taxes are included: In Ontario, your total loss settlement should typically include the applicable sales tax (HST) on the vehicle's value.
  • Confirm the deductible calculation: Ensure your deductible has been subtracted correctly and only once, based on your specific coverage.
  • Check your loan balance and GAP insurance: Compare the offer to what you owe on your loan. If you have GAP insurance or a depreciation waiver, check how it applies.
  • Understand your rental cutoff timing: Ask your adjuster exactly when your rental car coverage will end so you can plan accordingly without surprise out-of-pocket costs.
  • Ask about salvage options: If you are considering keeping the vehicle, ask what the owner-retained salvage value is and how it affects the final payout.

Red Flags to Review

While reviewing your offer, keep an eye out for these common issues that can affect your final settlement:

  • Wrong trim level: A base model is worth significantly less than a fully-loaded premium trim.
  • Wrong mileage: An extra zero or a missing digit can severely impact the depreciation curve.
  • Missing options: Factory-installed packages that add value should not be overlooked.
  • Comparables from far outside your area: Vehicle values differ by region; comparables should ideally reflect your local market.
  • Comparables that are not similar: A comparable vehicle should closely match yours in condition, mileage, and features.
  • Unexplained condition deductions: Large deductions for "wear and tear" should be itemized and clear.
  • Confusion between loan balance and ACV: Remember that the settlement is based on the vehicle's value, not what you owe the bank.

What Not to Overvalue

It is important to approach a total loss settlement with realistic expectations. Some factors that feel valuable to you may not translate to a higher settlement:

  • Recent maintenance: Standard upkeep like oil changes or new brakes does not usually add dollar-for-dollar value, as it is considered expected maintenance.
  • Emotional attachment: A vehicle's sentimental value is not factored into market value.
  • Dealership retail pricing: The sticker price at a dealership includes profit margins and prep fees that may not equal the vehicle's actual cash value.
  • Warranties and financing: Extended warranties, financing charges, and dealership add-ons are generally not included in the vehicle's ACV.

When to Ask Questions

If you find that the valuation contains clear errors, missing features, or incomplete information, you may be able to ask for a review. Valuation can often be reviewed if there are objective mistakes in the report. However, actual outcomes vary, and not every settlement is negotiable. Approach the conversation calmly and provide specific evidence, such as comparable listings or maintenance receipts, to support your questions.
